Your Poster Mission Planner

Your assignment is a tri-fold poster about deer in a forest habitat. This page walks through each panel so you can find all the facts quickly. The deadline is December 7, so use this site as your research library.

LEFT Panel: Discover Your Habitat (the Forest)

Use the Habitat page. It has everything the assignment asks for:

  • What it looks like: a deciduous forest with oak, maple, hickory, and birch trees, grasses, shrubs, and maybe a stream.
  • The weather: four seasons — warm rainy springs, hot summers, colorful crisp falls, and cold snowy winters.
  • Dangers: predators (coyotes, wolves, cougars, hawks) and harsh winter weather.
  • Where it is found: across North America — eastern U.S., Canada, and into Mexico.
  • Three bonus facts: the forest edge is a favorite spot, deer drink water every day, and one oak tree can drop hundreds of acorns.

RIGHT Panel: Meet Your Amazing Animal (the Deer)

Use the Species page. The facts the assignment needs are all there:

  • Animal: the white-tailed deer (or pick another deer species).
  • Appearance: brown coat, white tail, big ears, slender legs, and antlers on males.
  • Vertebrate? Yes — it has a backbone.
  • Mammal? Yes — it has fur, gives birth to live babies, and feeds them milk.
  • Warm-blooded? Yes — its body stays warm inside.
  • What it eats: plants only — leaves, grass, twigs, bark, acorns, and berries. (See the Food page.)
  • Don't forget: draw the deer on this side, just like the assignment says.

MIDDLE Panel: Built for Survival

Use the Survival Skills page for your paragraph. Pick two or three adaptations and explain how each helps the deer survive:

  • Speed — running up to 30 miles per hour to escape predators.
  • Camouflage — spotted fawns that blend into the leaves.
  • The white tail flash — a warning signal when frightened.
  • Radar-like ears — hearing danger from every direction.
  • The four-part stomach — digesting tough plants.

Your paragraph needs at least five complete sentences: a topic sentence, 2–3 adaptations with explanations, and a closing sentence. The Survival Skills page gives you ready-made sentences you can adapt.

Poster Title Ideas

  • “Deer in the Forest: My Wildlife Report”
  • “The Amazing White-Tailed Deer”
  • “A Day in the Life of a Deer”
  • “Forest Friend: How a Deer Survives”
  • “Speed, Spots, and Survival: The Deer Expedition”

Habitat Illustration Tips (Middle Panel Art)

Your drawing must be made by YOU — no printed pictures. Things to include:

  • Trees (oak or maple with colorful fall leaves)
  • Grass and wildflowers in a meadow edge
  • A stream, pond, or river
  • Fallen logs or rocks
  • Maybe distant hills or mountains
  • Season details, like snow in winter or red leaves in fall

Final Checklist (from your assignment)

  • I used a tri-fold poster board.
  • I researched and described my habitat.
  • I included three interesting habitat facts.
  • I researched and described my animal.
  • I included a picture of my animal.
  • I wrote a paragraph of at least five sentences explaining 2–3 survival adaptations.
  • I created my own handmade habitat illustration.
  • My poster has an exciting title and is neat, colorful, and organized.

Remember: cite your sources! Use the Sources & Books page to write down where you found your facts. You can also mention that you watched the science videos on the Videos page.
