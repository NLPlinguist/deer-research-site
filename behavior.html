How Do Deer Behave?

Deer have special habits and ways of acting. Understanding their behavior helps you learn how they survive. Here are the most important things to know about how deer live their daily lives.

Groups Called Herds

Many deer live in groups. A group of deer is called a herd. Living together helps them watch for danger and find food. A typical white-tailed deer group has about 6 to 12 animals. But sometimes deer are more alone — male deer often separate from the group during certain times of the year.

Day and Night

Deer are most active at dawn and dusk — the times right after sunrise and right before sunset. This is why people often spot deer early in the morning or in the evening. During the middle of the day, deer often rest in shaded, quiet places.

Staying Alert

Deer have great senses. Their ears move to catch sounds, their eyes are wide to see around them, and their noses can smell danger. When a deer smells or hears something scary, it freezes for a moment, then runs fast. This quick reaction helps deer escape predators.

Antlers and Mating

Only male deer (called bucks) grow antlers. Antlers are not horns — they are made of bone and they grow back every year. In the fall, male deer use antlers to compete for females. The strongest male usually wins the right to mate. Meet the different deer species →

Travel and Migration

Some deer travel long distances, especially in winter when food is hard to find. Caribou are famous for migrating — moving across huge areas to find food. Even white-tailed deer may move to different parts of their home when the seasons change.

How Deer Communicate

  • Body language: deer use their tail and body to show when they are scared or happy.
  • Sounds: elk and moose make loud calls, and fawns can make soft sounds to their mothers.
  • Smell: deer use scent to leave messages for other deer.
