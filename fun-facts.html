Deer Fun Facts

Here are some surprising facts about deer that you can use in your project. Each fact is something a third grader can remember and share with the class.

1. Deer Can Run Very Fast

White-tailed deer can run up to about 30 miles per hour. They use this speed to escape predators and to jump over obstacles in the forest.

2. Their Tails Are a Warning Light

When a white-tailed deer is scared, it raises its tail and shows a bright white flash. This tells other deer to be careful. It also helps the running deer confuse a predator.

3. Baby Deer Are Called Fawns

Baby deer are called fawns. Newborn fawns are born with white spots that help them hide in the leaves. For the first few weeks, a fawn stays hidden while its mother watches over it.

4. Antlers Grow and Fall Off

Male deer's antlers fall off in late winter and grow back in spring and summer. They are the largest in the fall. Antlers are made of a hard material that is actually a type of bone.

5. Deer Have Amazing Hearing

Deer's ears can move in different directions, like radar, to listen for sounds from all sides. This helps them hear danger coming from any direction.

6. They Love the Morning and Evening

Deer are most active at dawn and dusk. This is why people usually see deer early in the morning or in the evening.

7. Moose Are the Biggest

Moose are the largest deer in the world. A male moose can weigh more than 1,000 pounds and stand taller than a person. Compare all deer species →

8. Caribou Travel in Big Groups

Caribou live in huge herds and travel long distances. Some caribou herds can have thousands of animals moving together.

9. Deer Drink Water Every Day

Deer need fresh water every single day, so they always live near a stream, river, or pond. This is why a water source is an important part of their habitat.

10. Spots Help Fawns Hide

The white spots on a baby deer's back break up its outline, making it almost invisible among leaves and shadows. It is nature's camouflage suit.
