The Different Kinds of Deer

Not every deer looks the same. The family of deer animals is called Cervidae. In North America, you can find many different kinds. Let's meet them one by one. You can jump to any page using the menu at the top.

White-Tailed Deer

The white-tailed deer is one of the most common deer in North America. It has a brown coat in summer and a darker coat in winter. When it is scared, the deer flips up its tail to show a bright white flash — that is how it gets its name. Young white-tailed deer are born with white spots on their backs, which help them hide in the leaves.

Did you know? The white-tailed deer has two kinds of antlers. Young males grow antlers every year, shed them, and grow new ones in the fall. The antlers are made of a special bone-like material.

White-tailed deer are famous for being good swimmers and fast runners. They can run up to about 30 miles per hour. See more speed facts →

Mule Deer

Mule deer live mainly in the western United States. They look similar to white-tailed deer, but their antlers grow backward and upward, like the ears of a donkey (mule). That is why they are called “mule” deer. They often live in mountains and dry fields.

Mule deer travel in groups called herds. In the fall, the males (called bucks) fight for the right to mate with the females (called does). Learn about their behavior →

Black-Tailed Deer

The black-tailed deer lives in the Pacific Northwest, like Washington, Oregon, and California. It is a cousin of the white-tailed deer. Its tail is darker and it often has a light ring around its tail.

Elk

Elk are the largest deer in North America. A male elk can weigh more than 500 pounds! Elk are very loud — in the fall, males make a loud roaring sound called a bugle to attract females and warn other males. Elk live in forests and open fields, and they love to graze on grass.

Moose

Moose are the largest deer in the world and the largest in North America. They are dark, very strong, and love to swim and eat plants near lakes. Female moose give birth to one calf at a time. Moose have big, flat antlers that look almost like a crown.

Caribou

Caribou live in the cold northern places, like Alaska and Canada. Both males and females grow antlers, which is unusual for deer. Caribou travel together in large groups called herds and move long distances to find food.
